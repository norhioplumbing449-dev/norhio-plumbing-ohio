**1. Water Heaters: Knowing When the Tank Is Done**
Traditional tank water heaters typically last between 8 and 12 years, while tankless systems can run smoothly for 15 to 20 years with proper maintenance. If your unit is under eight years old and experiencing a minor issue—like a faulty thermostat, heating element, or pressure relief valve—a straightforward repair is usually the best route.

However, replacement is strongly recommended if you notice:
Rust-colored water coming exclusively from your hot water taps (a sign the interior tank is corroding).
Water pooling around the base of the tank due to an internal fracture.
Frequent breakdowns on a unit that is already past the 10-year mark.

**3. Faucets, Toilets, and Valves: Fixing Leaks Before They Spread**
A running toilet or a dripping kitchen faucet is almost always worth repairing if the fixture itself is in good condition. Replacing worn-out flappers, cartridges, O-rings, or shut-off valves is fast, affordable, and immediately stops water waste.

On the other hand, if you have older fixtures that constantly leak, lack replacement parts, or have visible mineral corrosion and cracks, upgrading to modern, water-saving fixtures will boost your home’s efficiency and appearance.
